How it works

From your question to the air

Three steps, and no account to create. This page is a mailbox for the show, not a forum.

Step one

You send it

One question or one topic pitch, in your own words. Add a theme if it helps place it, and a name only if you want credit on air.

Step two

The host reads and sorts it

Submissions are gathered for the arcs and conversations in development. A question that sharpens a planned conversation has the best chance of being used.

Step three

The best ones make the show

Selected questions are put to guests and selected topics shape episodes. Credit follows the name you gave, or stays anonymous if you gave none.

Season One themes

Pitch inside a theme, or outside all of them

Season One is organized as four arcs. A pitch that names its arc is easier to place, but a strong topic outside the arcs is still welcome. Tap a button below and the form above fills in the theme for you.

Arc I

The Codeveloping Lab

AI systems proposing hypotheses, screening compounds, and reading literatures no human team could finish. What a lab is, once part of its thinking is done by something it built.

Guiding question: When a discovery comes out of the loop, whose discovery is it?

Arc II

Minds, Extended and Edited

Brain computer interfaces, neural data, and tools that do not just assist thought but sit inside it. The territory where neuroethics began, now with AI on both sides of the interface.

Guiding question: Where does the scientist end and the instrument begin?

Arc III

Who Decides

Consent, oversight, and governance for research in which the researcher and the tool keep changing each other. What review boards, funders, and journals can actually see.

Guiding question: Who consents, and to what, when the experiment changes the experimenter?

Arc IV

The Scientist in the Mirror

Judgment, skill, and identity after years inside the loop. What senior scientists notice changing in themselves, and what trainees never get the chance to build.

Guiding question: What should the loop never be allowed to optimize away?

Before you send

What makes a submission usable

Strong submissions

  • One thing at a time. One question, or one topic with one clear angle.
  • Specific, not general. A lab, a device, a decision, a moment you have seen or lived.
  • Grounded in the loop. It connects scientists, their AI tools, and how each changes the other.
  • Open, not settled. A question a thoughtful guest could genuinely disagree about.

How your words may be used

Questions and pitches sent here may be read, quoted, or paraphrased on the show and in its show notes, with the name you provide or as a listener when you provide none.

A submission may be shortened for length or clarity on air. Sending a topic is a gift to the show, not a claim on an episode, and selection is the host decision.

Please do not send personal medical details, private information about other people, or material you do not have the right to share.
